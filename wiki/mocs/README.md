# 🗺️ Maps of Content (MOC)

> Thematic **entry points** into the wiki, inspired by Zettelkasten's Maps of Content.

A MOC is **not** where knowledge lives — it's a curated navigator that routes you to the right entities, concepts and syntheses on a given topic. Think of it as a hand-picked table of contents for a theme.

---

## Why MOCs?

The wiki has 3 surfaces (`entities/`, `concepts/`, `syntheses/`) plus auto-generated backlinks. For recurring topics (e.g. "Italy", "Payments", "E-invoicing"), jumping across all 3 surfaces is noisy. A MOC collapses that into **one read**.

Good MOC candidates:
- Topics you re-open weekly (e.g., your country expansion, your main project, a cross-cutting domain)
- Cross-surface themes where the story only makes sense when all pieces are assembled
- Anything your manager might ask "where are we on X?"

Not good MOC candidates:
- One-off meetings (→ `syntheses/`)
- A definition (→ `concepts/`)

---

## Rules

1. **Thin pointers, not duplication** — a MOC links, it does not explain. If you catch yourself writing paragraphs, promote the content to a proper concept/synthesis page and link back.
2. **Curated, not exhaustive** — skip noise. A MOC is opinionated navigation.
3. **English only** (wiki convention).
4. **YAML front-matter mandatory** (`last_reviewed`). Bump when reorganising.
5. **Structure** — use the sections in [`_template.md`](_template.md). Feel free to drop sections that don't apply.
6. **Open questions** section is optional but encouraged — MOCs surface what's missing.

---

## Filename convention

`MOC_<Topic>.md` — e.g. `MOC_E-invoicing.md`, `MOC_Payments.md`, `MOC_Italy.md`.

Topic in PascalCase or Title-Case with hyphens.
